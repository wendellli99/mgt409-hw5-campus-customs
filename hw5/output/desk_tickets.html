What would have been simpler with one agent and tools?

Ticket 102 is a good candidate. One agent could read the lease and checking evidence, prepare the exact rent approval, stop for the human, then verify the ledger and draft the landlord response. Splitting Facilities and Accounting makes ownership visible, but the task has a short, mostly deterministic state transition.

Ticket 101 also repeats some facts when Boss, Inventory and Accounting each inspect the same ticket. A single agent could maintain the stockout, invoice blocker and vendor lead time in one context, prepare the invoice, then save the follow-up and response after approval. Separate specialists are more useful if stock allocation or supplier alternatives become substantially more complex.

For ticket 103, one agent with get_inventory and get_financial_context could compare stock, unit cost, requested quantity and the final $160.00 balance, then draft an honest smaller/deferred offer. The multi-agent route provides an inspectable financial review, but forwarding the same calculations among agents adds requests and makes mistakes harder to attribute. In either architecture, human approval and MCP cash safeguards still belong in deterministic tools.

Three new problems this team can solve with these tools

These are new instances of supported ticket types, assuming the request and referenced invoice, lease, or stock record already exist in the shop database. The current tools do not provide new-ticket intake or external message delivery.

  1. Another customer request for a sold-out size tied to an existing unpaid reprint invoice. Inventory can inspect that SKU and size; Accounting can assess and prepare the existing invoice payment; after a human approves, the team can save an internal restock follow-up and an honest customer draft. This reuses the constrained path exercised on 101 and still does not claim physical fulfillment.
  2. A new notice about a lease payment due soon. Facilities can read the recorded lease, Accounting can check cash and prepare its exact rent payment, and the human can approve. The ledger and next_due transition used on 102 provide an auditable outcome and a locally saved landlord draft.
  3. A student organization asks whether a smaller recorded-stock order or a different proposed discount is feasible. Inventory and Accounting can compare actual stock, prices, total margin and incremental replenishment cash, then Customer Service can save an offer or decline draft. The reasoning used on 103 supports this decision; these tools do not establish a discount policy, collect money, reserve stock, or send the offer.

Three new problems the current tools cannot solve

  1. A supplier shipment is lost and the customer needs a confirmed delivery date. schedule_restock stores an internal lead-time estimate, not a carrier tracking record or goods receipt. The app would need supplier confirmation, purchase-order shipment updates, carrier tracking, and receipt tools, with a Fulfillment or Supplier Operations agent. Ticket 101 is the concrete reason those distinctions matter.
  2. A landlord disputes a payment or the shop needs to renegotiate its lease. The present ledger can show the internal rent action from 102 but cannot read bank settlement, send evidence to the landlord, or change lease terms. Bank reconciliation, document retrieval, landlord messaging, and a human-reviewed lease amendment workflow would be needed, with a Facilities/Lease Administration specialist and Accounting for settlement verification.
  3. The Yale AI Club accepts a 20-hoodie quote and wants to pay a deposit to fund replenishment. Ticket 103 cannot create cash from a hypothetical sale. The app needs quote acceptance, customer invoicing, payment collection, deposit accounting, inventory reservation, and order fulfillment tools, with Sales/Order Management and Payment/Reconciliation agents plus human oversight. Only those recorded deposits could change the current cash constraint.
